The self-built homes of Santiago are precarious and ever changing. They are, always and at once, being and becoming.

圣地亚哥那些由居民自行建造的房屋脆弱不稳，而且始终处于变化之中。它们总是同时处于存在与生成之中。

Few problems feel more pressing today than housing. Across the world, access to affordable, stable, dignified housing has become one of the defining challenges of our time, in wealthy capitals and struggling peripheries alike. According to UN-Habitat, more than 2.8 billion people – roughly 35 per cent of the global population – lack access to adequate housing. Rents rise faster than wages; ownership recedes as an aspiration for younger generations; entire neighbourhoods are reshaped by speculation and displacement. The question of who gets to live where, and on what terms, sits at the heart of contemporary urban life.

如今，似乎很少有什么问题比住房更加迫切。在世界各地，无论是在富裕的首都，还是在艰难发展的边缘地区，能否获得价格可负担、稳定而有尊严的住房，都已成为我们这个时代最具代表性的挑战之一。联合国人居署指出，超过 28 亿人——约占全球人口的 35%——无法获得适足住房。房租上涨的速度快于工资；对于年轻一代而言，拥有住房正越来越不再是一个可实现的愿望；整个街区也在投机活动和人口迁移中被重新塑造。谁能够住在哪里、又以什么条件居住，已经成为当代城市生活的核心问题。

This is not a new problem. In *The Housing Question* (1872), Friedrich Engels identified it as a structural consequence of industrial capitalism: as workers flooded into cities, the market and the state proved incapable of providing, or unwilling to provide, shelter for all. From that moment, housing became a political question. Over the following century, governments across the developed world came to assume that the solution lay in their hands: through public construction, regulation, subsidy, and the organised machinery of mortgage markets and mass homeownership. Housing was a problem to be solved – technically, bureaucratically and financially. Today, that problem has only deepened: a global crisis of affordability and tenure grips cities across the world, and housing is drawn ever further into the circuits of finance and speculation.

这并不是一个新问题。弗里德里希·恩格斯在 1872 年的《论住宅问题》中，将它视为工业资本主义的一种结构性后果：随着工人大量涌入城市，市场和国家既无力、也往往无意为所有人提供住所。从那以后，住房便成为一个政治问题。在接下来的一个世纪里，发达国家的政府逐渐认为解决方案掌握在自己手中：通过公共住房建设、监管、补贴，以及有组织的抵押贷款市场和大规模住房所有权体系来解决问题。住房被视作一个可以从技术、官僚行政和金融层面加以解决的问题。如今，这个问题只变得更加严重：住房可负担性和居住权保障的全球性危机笼罩着世界各地的城市，而住房也越来越深地被卷入金融和投机的循环。

Yet across much of the 20th century, in the rapidly urbanising cities of Latin America, Africa and Asia, the state-led housing paradigm never fully took hold. Cities grew faster than any state or real-estate market could manage. Millions gained access to housing – and to the city itself – not through the formal mechanisms of policy and planning, but by occupying land, building by hand and gradually, generation by generation, turning bare earth into home, in ways that the state variously resisted, tolerated, regularised or actively supported. In countless such cities, self-built houses and neighbourhoods were not an exception to the urban order; they were intrinsic to how the city was made.

然而，在 20 世纪的大部分时间里，在拉丁美洲、非洲和亚洲那些迅速城市化的城市中，由国家主导的住房模式从未真正全面建立起来。城市增长的速度超过了任何国家机构或房地产市场所能应对的程度。数以百万计的人并不是通过正式的政策与规划机制获得住房——乃至进入城市本身——而是通过占据土地、亲手建造房屋，一代又一代地逐渐将裸露的土地变成家园。面对这些做法，国家有时抵制，有时容忍，有时将其合法化，也有时积极提供支持。在无数这样的城市里，自建住房和自建社区并不是城市秩序中的例外；它们本身就是城市得以形成的内在组成部分。

The precariousness, informality and lack of top-down planning that characterise access to shelter in the cities of the Global South may well justify seeing them as a failure – a sign of governments’ and societies’ inability to adequately resolve the housing question. And yet, somewhat paradoxically, it is precisely among self-built environments that housing can be seen from an angle rarely present in mainstream debate, and nonetheless fundamental: that any serious attempt to understand how societies provide a place to live must first ask what it means to live somewhere at all, and what role the house itself plays in that. In simple words, that behind every housing problem lies a dwelling problem. Behind the pervasive technical illusion of the house as object, commodity and asset, the building-dwelling bond tends to disappear. By contrast, in self-building, that relationship remains exposed and alive, woven into the life that the house makes possible, and that makes the house possible in return. Two decades of fieldwork in the urban margins of Chile’s capital Santiago have led me to see, in this little-by-little, intergenerational, unfinished way of making one’s own house, an experience of building that has something to teach us about the art of dwelling.

全球南方城市中的住房获取往往具有不稳定性、非正规性，并缺乏自上而下的规划，因此我们完全可以把这种状况看作一种失败——它表明政府和社会无力妥善解决住房问题。然而颇具悖论意味的是，恰恰是在这些自建环境中，我们能够从一种主流讨论中很少出现、却至关重要的角度重新看待住房：任何真正严肃地试图理解一个社会如何为人们提供居所的努力，都必须首先追问，住在某个地方究竟意味着什么，而房屋本身在其中又发挥着怎样的作用。简单地说，每一个住房问题背后，都存在一个栖居问题。在那种将房屋视为物件、商品和资产的普遍技术性幻象背后，建造与栖居之间的联系往往消失不见。相比之下，在自建过程中，这种关系始终暴露在外、保持鲜活：它被编织进房屋所成就的生活之中，而这样的生活反过来也使房屋的存在成为可能。过去二十年，我一直在智利首都圣地亚哥的城市边缘地区进行田野调查。这让我逐渐意识到，这种一点一点、跨越世代、始终未完成的自建住房方式，体现出一种建造经验，而这种经验能够教给我们一些有关“栖居的艺术”的东西。

I came to the question of the house long before I had any concepts for it. Victoria worked in my family’s home when I was a child and, for as long as I can remember, I knew her through the story of her own house – a story I first overheard in her conversations with my mother, and which years later, when I began my first fieldwork on domestic life in Santiago, became the first I set out to study, and the one I have returned to ever since. Her house was Victoria’s favourite subject of conversation. Whenever we talked, it would intrude everywhere: in anecdotes, in complaints, in memories, even in the most ordinary exchanges about almost anything.

早在我拥有任何可以理解“房屋”这一问题的概念之前，我就已经接触到了它。维多利亚在我小时候曾在我家工作。从我记事起，我就是通过她自己那栋房子的故事来认识她的——最初，我是在她与母亲的谈话中无意间听到这些故事的。多年以后，当我第一次在圣地亚哥开展关于家庭生活的田野调查时，它成为我最先着手研究的故事，此后我也一次又一次回到这个故事。她的房子是维多利亚最喜欢谈论的话题。每当我们交谈，它总会闯进谈话的各个角落：轶事里、抱怨里、回忆里，甚至关于几乎任何事情的最寻常闲聊里，都少不了它的身影。

Victoria was already an older woman when I first interviewed her in 2007, early in what would become ongoing fieldwork on domestic life in Santiago. She had retired some years earlier, after a lifetime as a domestic worker, due to advanced diabetes. Born in a very poor village of seaweed gatherers, Victoria ran away as a child from the foster family her birth mother had placed her with. She barely attended school, and while still an adolescent she joined her life to the man who would become her husband. With him she had six children – the first before she turned 15; the youngest, disabled, already in his 40s – and she became a great-grandmother before 50.

2007 年我第一次采访维多利亚时，她已经上了年纪。那时，我刚刚开始后来持续多年的圣地亚哥家庭生活田野调查。由于糖尿病病情严重，她在几年前已经退休，在此之前，她一生都从事家庭佣工工作。维多利亚出生在一个极其贫困、以采集海藻为生的村庄。小时候，她从生母将她托付给的寄养家庭中逃了出来。她几乎没有上过学，还是少女时便与后来成为她丈夫的男人一起生活。他们共有六个孩子——第一个孩子出生时她还不到 15 岁；最小的孩子身有残障，如今也已经四十多岁——而维多利亚自己还不到 50 岁便已经成了曾祖母。

If many of the topics we discussed were, for her, a source of complaint – particularly those concerning her health and the everyday tensions among her relatives – Victoria seemed to take a particular, almost contagious pleasure in describing the smallest details of how the house had been built from the moment they arrived, when the plot was ‘nothing but bare earth’, as well as its successive transformations and its ongoing plans, improvements and repairs.

我们谈论的许多话题都会引起她的抱怨——尤其是她的健康状况，以及亲属之间日常发生的种种摩擦。但只要谈到那栋房子，维多利亚似乎就会显露出一种格外强烈、甚至具有感染力的快乐。她喜欢描述他们刚来到这块地时——当时那里“除了光秃秃的泥土什么也没有”——房子是如何一点一点建起来的，喜欢讲述此后一次又一次的改造，以及那些至今仍在进行中的计划、改善和修缮。

I was struck by her meticulous attention to materiality and to specific quantities – a sack of cement, two sheets of corrugated zinc, 10 metal bars for the gate – the exact inputs she and her husband were constantly using. Certain words began to acquire a new meaning for me in this context. They were ‘closing’ (*cerrando*) the plot – building the wall that separates the house from the street. They needed to ‘line’ (*forrar*) the interior wooden walls – adding a second layer of boards to insulate them better. She spoke of needing to ‘raise’ (*levantar*) the kitchen floor, of building a terrace; at one point she had had to ‘raise the *radier*’ – to cover the concrete slab with an additional layer of cement. She dreamed of ‘finishing’ (*terminar*) the bathroom, which she had never managed to build entirely in material – by which she meant cement and brick, as opposed to wood, which was not considered a proper material because it was not as solid and durable. These were often long-term and discontinuous processes, unfolding in stages and marked by pauses and resumptions.

最令我印象深刻的是，她对物质材料和具体数量的关注极其细致——一袋水泥、两张波纹锌板、十根做大门用的金属条——她会准确说出自己和丈夫不断投入其中的每一样东西。在这样的语境里，一些词对我来说开始获得新的含义。他们在“封起来”（*cerrando*）这块地——也就是修建把房屋与街道分隔开的围墙。他们需要给室内木墙“包一层”（*forrar*）——再加上一层木板，以获得更好的保温效果。她谈到需要把厨房地面“抬高”（*levantar*），还想建一个露台；有一次，她不得不“抬高 *radier*”——也就是在混凝土板上再铺一层水泥。她梦想把浴室“做完”（*terminar*），因为她始终没有能力将浴室全部用所谓真正的“材料”建造起来——这里的“材料”指水泥和砖，而不是木头；木头因为不够坚固耐久，并不被认为是真正的建筑材料。这些工程往往持续很长时间，而且并不连续：它们分阶段展开，不断暂停，又不断重新开始。

The neverending story of the making of Victoria’s house was intimately intertwined with another story that unfolded in our conversations: the gradual and constantly reconfigured settlement of her children’s families. Over time, as many as four generations lived under Victoria’s roof – whether by rearranging existing rooms or by adding new ones in the backyard and on top of the house. Such intertwining of materiality and relatedness becomes especially clear in a conversation with one of her daughters. A few years earlier, after three decades in her mother’s home, she had moved to a small, rented house behind another property nearby, in order to make space for her own grandchildren. ‘If you have problems,’ she said to me, ‘the only place to go is your mother’s. If my husband tells me to leave, I come back here. You know that your parents will always open the door to you. My parents have never closed their doors to their children – never.’

维多利亚那栋房子永不停歇的建造故事，与我们谈话中逐渐展开的另一个故事紧密交织在一起：她的子女及其家庭如何一点一点迁入，而居住安排又如何不断重新调整。随着时间推移，最多曾有四代人同时生活在维多利亚的屋檐下——有时是重新安排已有的房间，有时是在后院或者房屋上方再加盖新的空间。物质建造与亲属关系之间的这种交织，在我与她一个女儿的谈话中表现得尤其清楚。几年前，在母亲家中住了三十年之后，为了给自己的孙辈腾出空间，她搬进了附近另一处房产后面的一间小租住房。“如果你出了什么问题，”她对我说，“唯一能去的地方就是你妈妈家。如果我丈夫让我走，我就回到这里。你知道，你的父母永远都会为你开门。我的父母从来没有对自己的孩子关过门——从来没有。”

And yet the same house that never closes its door can also close in. Victoria, who took such pleasure in recounting how it was built, spoke of those years in another register too: ‘crowded together’ (*todos amontonados*), ‘you cannot fight for your life, fight for yourself’. She still dreamed of the terrace, the small garden, the raised kitchen floor she never had room to finish. Here one glimpses the enduring strength of the moral expectation to welcome one’s children, and the extent to which home-leaving is understood as reversible. The house appears as something pliable – and that pliability cuts both ways. It can crowd and exhaust as readily as it can shelter; but because it is never finished, it holds the conflict open, as something that might yet be eased by a room added at the back, a wall moved, a life rearranged. A ‘definitive house’ forecloses that wager. The self-built one keeps it alive – for better and for worse.

然而，这栋永远不会把门关上的房子，也可能反过来让人感到逼仄窒息。维多利亚在讲述房子的建造过程时总是兴致勃勃，但谈到那些年月时，她也会换一种语调：“全都挤在一起”（*todos amontonados*），“你根本没办法为自己的生活争取，没办法为自己活。”她仍然梦想着那个露台、那个小花园，还有始终没有足够空间完成的抬高后的厨房地面。在这里，我们可以看到一种道德期待具有多么持久的力量：父母理应接纳自己的孩子，而离开父母的家，也在很大程度上被理解为一种随时可以逆转的状态。房屋似乎是一种可以弯曲、调整的东西——但这种可塑性具有双重作用。它既能够提供庇护，也同样能够制造拥挤、令人疲惫；可正因为房屋永远没有真正完工，冲突也就始终没有被彻底封死：也许未来在后面加一个房间、挪动一堵墙、重新安排一种生活，问题便可以得到缓解。一栋“最终住房”会关闭这种可能性，而自建住房则让它始终保持开放——无论结果是好是坏。

Victoria’s story is part of a broader reality among poor urban populations across Latin America, where roughly a quarter to a third of urban residents have lived in informal housing conditions as cities expanded through the late 20th and early 21st century. In Santiago, much of what is now the pericentral belt took shape between the 1960s and ’80s through a precarious process of informal occupation of marginal land and later state-regularised *poblaciones* that shaped the city’s growth. These are neighbourhoods slowly and collectively assembled by hand over multiple generations – their houses, streets and public spaces still bearing the marks of that history, as do the family configurations and forms of local sociability, as well as civic and political participation, that took shape within them.

维多利亚的故事只是拉丁美洲贫困城市人口更广泛现实的一部分。随着城市在 20 世纪后期至 21 世纪初不断扩张，大约四分之一至三分之一的城市居民曾生活在非正规住房条件下。在圣地亚哥，如今环绕城市中心的大片区域，都是在 20 世纪 60 至 80 年代逐渐形成的：人们最初以不稳定的方式非正规占据边缘土地，后来这些 *poblaciones* 社区又得到国家的正规化处理，由此塑造了城市的扩张。这些社区经过几代人的时间，由居民缓慢地、集体地亲手建造出来——房屋、街道和公共空间至今仍保留着这段历史留下的痕迹；在这里形成的家庭结构、地方社会交往形式，以及公民和政治参与方式，同样带着这种历史的印记。

Although it may be technically correct to consider the self-built house as merely one particular residential typology among others, I have come to think otherwise. I arrived with the vocabulary of deficit – informality, overcrowding, the housing unit to be counted and completed – and the field slowly took it apart. In all their precariousness – and perhaps partly because of it – self-built houses reveal some conditions without which a place cannot truly be lived as a house. These are conditions just as present, though far harder to see, in the finished and affluent houses I have also studied across Santiago. This is why I see the self-built dwelling not as a type but as an archetype of housing – not because it is exemplary, but because it is revealing: in it, what every house already is becomes visible.

从技术分类来看，把自建住房仅仅视为众多住宅类型中的一种或许并没有错，但我后来逐渐产生了不同的看法。进入田野时，我带着一套关于“缺陷”的词汇——非正规、过度拥挤、需要统计并最终完成的住房单元——而田野经验却一点一点拆解了这套语言。尽管自建住房充满不稳定性——甚至可能恰恰部分因为这种不稳定性——它们揭示出了一些基本条件：如果缺少这些条件，一个地方便无法真正作为一栋“房子”被人生活其中。我在圣地亚哥研究过的那些已经完工、富裕的住宅里，同样存在这些条件，只不过在那里更加难以看见。正因为如此，我并不把自建住宅视作一种住房“类型”，而把它看作住房的一种原型——并不是因为它值得效仿，而是因为它具有揭示性：在这里，每一栋房屋原本已经是什么，变得清晰可见。

I found a philosophical grounding for this intuition in Martin Heidegger’s essay ‘Building Dwelling Thinking’ (1951), in which he reflects on the deeper relation between dwelling and building in order to illuminate what he calls the plight of dwelling in our time. The text was first delivered as a lecture at the Darmstadt Colloquium, a gathering of architects, philosophers and intellectuals convened in the context of the postwar reconstruction of a defeated and devastated Germany. The colloquium took place at a moment of intense pressure to industrialise construction and address a massive housing shortage. The question at stake was whether reconstruction should be guided primarily by technical efficiency and standardisation, or whether the problem ran deeper – touching on the very meaning of dwelling in the modern age. What Heidegger diagnosed in relation to industrialised building has since been intensified by the financialisation of housing.

我在马丁·海德格尔 1951 年的文章《建造、栖居、思考》（*Building Dwelling Thinking*）中，为这种直觉找到了哲学基础。在这篇文章中，他通过思考栖居与建造之间更深层的关系，试图阐明他所谓我们这个时代的“栖居之困”。这篇文章最初是海德格尔在达姆施塔特研讨会上的演讲。当时，战败并遭受严重破坏的德国正在进行战后重建，建筑师、哲学家和知识分子汇聚于此展开讨论。当时社会承受着巨大的压力：必须实现建筑工业化，以应对极其严重的住房短缺。真正的问题在于，重建是否应当主要由技术效率和标准化原则来主导，抑或问题其实更加深刻——它触及现代时代中“栖居”本身究竟意味着什么。海德格尔针对工业化建造所诊断的问题，此后又随着住房金融化而进一步加剧。

Heidegger’s argument begins by acknowledging the widespread – and understandable – urgency to build large numbers of houses. Construction becomes the object of diagnoses, planning schemes, industrial coordination and commercial enterprise, all directed toward the efficient production of dwelling units. In this framework, building appears as a technical and economic task, and houses as functional objects designed to provide shelter. Against this backdrop, Heidegger shifts the question. However acute the housing shortage, he writes, the ‘real plight of dwelling’ does not lie simply in the lack of houses, but in the fact that we have yet to learn how to dwell. Building efficiently doesn’t guarantee that what is built can be truly lived in.

海德格尔的论证首先承认，人们迫切需要大规模建造住房，这种需求既普遍存在，也完全可以理解。建造于是成为诊断、规划方案、工业协调和商业活动共同作用的对象，而这一切都以高效率生产住宅单元为目标。在这样的框架中，建造表现为一种技术和经济任务，而房屋则是为了提供遮蔽功能而设计的功能性物件。在这样的背景下，海德格尔改变了提问的方向。他写道，无论住房短缺有多么严重，“真正的栖居之困”都不仅仅在于缺少房屋，而在于我们尚未学会如何栖居。高效率地建造，并不能保证建出来的东西真正能够成为人生活其中的居所。

Within the technical paradigm, dwelling and building appear as sequential: we build in order to dwell. Heidegger reverses the assumption. We build because we dwell. This is not simply a reversal of cause and effect. It is a more radical claim: dwelling has ontological priority. It names the fundamental way in which mortals are upon the earth. He grounds this claim in language itself: the modern German *bauen* (‘to build’) derives from the Old High German *buan*, which originally meant to remain in a place – to dwell – and is related to *bin*, ‘I am’. At its root, building already belongs to dwelling. The priority of dwelling is therefore ontological: it names the fundamental way in which mortals are upon the earth.

在技术范式中，栖居与建造似乎具有一种先后顺序：我们为了栖居，所以先进行建造。海德格尔将这个假设倒转过来。我们之所以建造，是因为我们首先已经在栖居。这并不仅仅是在因果关系上进行简单的颠倒，而是一个更加激进的主张：栖居具有本体论上的优先性。它指称的是凡人存在于大地之上的根本方式。海德格尔从语言本身出发为这一主张寻找依据：现代德语中的 *bauen*（“建造”）来自古高地德语 *buan*，这个词最初意味着停留在一个地方——也就是栖居——并且与 *bin*（“我是”）存在词源联系。因此从词根意义上说，建造原本就属于栖居。栖居的优先性因而是一种本体论上的优先性：它指的是凡人在大地上存在的根本方式。

In much of the contemporary world, houses are delivered as finished products – ready to be inhabited, financed and exchanged. Within this technical paradigm, the house exists as a completed object, externally related to those who later occupy it. Life enters a structure already defined.

在当代世界的大部分地区，房屋都作为一种已经完工的产品被交付——随时可以入住、融资和交易。在这种技术范式中，房屋作为一个已经完成的对象而存在，它与后来住进去的人之间只具有一种外在关系。生活进入的是一个早已被规定好的结构。

By contrast, in contexts of self-construction, a house’s becoming is interwoven with the events that shape its inhabitants’ lives – expected or unforeseen, welcome or distressing. These houses remain open and adaptable. They are built slowly alongside the making of kinship and personhood. Floors are raised when money allows; walls are lined later; rooms are added as children grow, couples separate, grandchildren arrive. Small piles of cement, timber or corrugated sheets often sit in the corners of a plot – materials acquired when possible, signs of a future still under construction. Through an irregular rhythm of gifts, loans and shared labour, houses and persons become durably linked. Because the house remains unfinished, it becomes the horizon within which aspirations and frustrations, projects and setbacks take shape.

相比之下，在自建住房的环境中，一栋房屋的“生成”会与塑造居住者人生的各种事件交织在一起——无论这些事件是预料之中的还是突如其来的，是令人欣喜的还是令人痛苦的。这些房屋始终保持开放，并能够不断适应变化。它们在亲属关系和人格逐渐形成的同时，也被缓慢地建造出来。有钱的时候把地面抬高；以后再给墙加上一层板材；孩子长大了、夫妻分开了、孙辈出生了，就再增加新的房间。在一块宅地的角落里，经常会堆放着少量水泥、木材或波纹板——都是有条件时一点一点买来的材料，也是某个仍处于建造之中的未来留下的迹象。通过礼物、借贷和共同劳动这种并不规则的节奏，房屋与人之间逐渐形成持久的联系。正因为房屋始终没有完工，它也就成为一个地平线：人的愿望与挫败、计划与阻碍，都在这个地平线之中逐渐获得形状。

The contrast with Chile’s social housing regime since the 1980s is striking. In official jargon, the dwelling delivered by the state is a *vivienda definitiva* – a ‘definitive house’. Designed as complete units with limited scope for expansion, these houses are granted once in a lifetime, regardless of a family’s position in the life cycle. Residential trajectories are anchored to a single institutional event. Those who have already received a subsidy are even described as ‘marked’.

这与智利自 20 世纪 80 年代以来实行的社会住房制度形成了鲜明对比。在官方术语中，由国家交付的住宅被称为 *vivienda definitiva*——也就是“最终住房”。这些住宅被设计成已经完成的单元，可扩建空间十分有限，而且一个人一生通常只能获得一次，无论一个家庭当时正处于怎样的生命周期阶段。一个家庭此后的居住轨迹，由此被固定在一次单一的制度性事件之上。已经获得过住房补贴的人，甚至会被形容为已经被“标记”过。

A second dimension becomes visible in the way houses and persons are bound together through kinship and co-residence. In the technical paradigm, the house exists first; inhabitants enter later. In self-construction, this separation collapses. The history of a house’s construction is inseparable from the relationships that made it possible. Materials circulate among relatives; labour is lent and returned; rooms are added not only to accommodate bodies but to sustain bonds. But the same circulation can be refused, and to refuse it is to refuse the bond. When Victoria began to ‘close’ her plot – to build the wall onto the street – she asked her son-in-law for a sack of cement, or two. He sent word back that he could put nothing on a plot that was not his. ‘That was a hard blow,’ she said. To withhold the cement was to withhold the relationship.

第二个层面，则体现在房屋与人如何通过亲属关系和共同居住联系在一起。在技术范式中，房屋首先独立存在，居住者随后才进入其中。而在自建住房中，这种分离瓦解了。一栋房子的建造史无法与那些使建造成为可能的人际关系分开。建筑材料在亲属之间流转；劳动被借出，再以劳动偿还；增加房间不仅是为了容纳更多人的身体，也是为了维系人与人之间的纽带。然而，这种流转也可能遭到拒绝，而拒绝这种流转，本身就是拒绝关系。当维多利亚开始把自己的宅地“封起来”——也就是沿街修建围墙——时，她向女婿要一袋或两袋水泥。女婿托人回应说，一块不属于自己的土地，他什么东西也不会放进去。“那对我的打击很大。”她说。不给水泥，也就是拒绝那段关系。

In Victoria’s case, children and grandchildren moved in and out as circumstances required. What might appear as overcrowding from the outside was locally understood as obligation and continuity. Building does not merely provide a setting for kinship; it gives it material form. To add a room ‘at the back’ or ‘above’ is not only a spatial act but a relational one. Distance and proximity, autonomy and dependence, conflict and reconciliation are built into the arrangement of rooms, thresholds and passages. Over time, several dwellings may differentiate within what was once a single house – small ‘independent’ units drawn from the original structure. The plot becomes a constellation of related domestic spaces, linked by shared histories of construction and inhabitation. The house is not simply where relatedness unfolds; it is one of the means through which it is realised.

在维多利亚家中，孩子和孙辈会根据现实情况不断搬进来、再搬出去。从外部观察者的角度看，这也许属于过度拥挤，但在当地人的理解中，它意味着义务以及亲属关系的延续。建造并不仅仅为亲属关系提供一个发生的场所；它还赋予这种关系以物质形式。在房屋“后面”或者“上面”再加一个房间，并不只是一个空间行为，同时也是一种关系行为。远与近、自主与依赖、冲突与和解，都被建造进房间、门槛和通道的具体布局之中。随着时间推移，原本的一栋住宅内部可能逐渐分化出若干居住单元——从原有结构中划分出来的小型“独立”住宅。整块宅地于是变成一个由相互关联的家庭空间组成的星座，而这些空间又被共同的建造史和居住史连接起来。房屋并不只是亲属关系展开的地方；它本身就是使这种关系得以实现的手段之一。

A third dimension concerns the relation between house and land. In many of Santiago’s peripheries, the house begins not with construction but with occupation. Before there was a wall or roof, there was a plot described as ‘nothing but bare earth’. The foundational gesture was taking and holding a piece of land.

第三个层面涉及房屋与土地之间的关系。在圣地亚哥许多城市边缘地区，一栋房子的起点并不是“建造”，而是“占据”。在墙壁和屋顶出现之前，首先存在的是一块被描述为“除了裸土什么都没有”的土地。最初、也是奠定一切基础的行动，是取得并守住一块土地。

Rosa’s story makes this visible in another way. Her wooden house stood on stilts embedded in the slopes of the Chicauma hill. Years earlier, someone had offered her the plot precisely because no one else wanted it: settling there meant ‘*ganársela al cerro*’ – winning the ground from the hill. ‘Every day I would come up here,’ she told me, ‘and clear a little ground; then, when my husband got back from work, he would come and do a little more.’ This is how houses are made here: *de a poquito*, little by little. A neighbour gave her a small shack, three metres by three, to set down and ‘secure the plot’. Each winter, heavy rains could undo what had been built. The domestication of the hill was never definitive.

罗莎的故事以另一种方式呈现了这一点。她的木屋由支柱撑起，支柱深深嵌入奇考马山的斜坡之中。多年前，有人之所以愿意把这块地给她，恰恰因为根本没有其他人想要它：在这里安家意味着 *ganársela al cerro*——从山坡手里“赢得”一块土地。“我每天都会上来这里，”她告诉我，“先清出一点地；等我丈夫下班回来以后，他再过来多清一点。”这里的房屋就是这样造出来的：*de a poquito*，一点一点地。一个邻居送给她一间三米见方的小棚屋，让她先把它放在这里，以便“守住这块地”。每到冬天，暴雨都有可能毁掉此前已经完成的工程。对这片山坡的驯化，从来都不是一劳永逸的。

![View from the hill](https://images.aeonmedia.co/user_image_upload/5165/sz-view-no-dog.jpg?width=3840&quality=75&format=auto)

To level the ground, mark boundaries and raise a first shelter is not merely preparatory to building; it is already an act of dwelling. What was once ‘pure earth’ becomes, through repeated acts of care and repair, a lived territory. The plot carries the memory of that founding gesture and, with it, a moral claim to belonging. Here, building still follows dwelling. But, elsewhere, the order has shifted. If Heidegger diagnosed the instrumentalisation of building and dwelling under the technical paradigm, financialisation pushes that logic further still. Over recent decades, housing has been drawn into the circuits of global finance: mortgages are bundled and traded, developments structured as investment vehicles, entire neighbourhoods acquired as investment portfolios. The house becomes an *asset*. Even its earlier status as a means to dwelling fades. Whether it is inhabited becomes secondary, sometimes irrelevant. This is a further step of abstraction: the house is valued not for the life it hosts, but for the returns it promises.

平整土地、标出边界、搭起第一间住所，并不仅仅是在为正式建房做准备；这些行动本身就已经构成了栖居。原先只是“纯粹泥土”的地方，通过一次又一次照料和修缮，逐渐成为一个被生活过的领地。这块土地保存着最初那个奠基行动的记忆，而这种记忆也伴随着一种对于“属于这里”的道德主张。在这里，建造仍然发生在栖居之后。但在其他地方，这一顺序已经发生改变。如果说海德格尔所诊断的是技术范式之下建造与栖居的工具化，那么住房金融化又把这种逻辑向前推进了一步。过去几十年中，住房被卷入全球金融循环：抵押贷款被打包并交易，房地产开发项目被设计成投资工具，整个街区被当作投资组合收购。房屋变成了一项*资产*。甚至它早先作为“栖居手段”的地位也在逐渐消退。一栋房子究竟有没有人居住，变成了次要问题，有时甚至完全无关紧要。这是更进一步的抽象化：房屋的价值不再取决于它所承载的生活，而取决于它承诺带来的收益。

The consequences are visible. Across the globe, millions struggle to secure stable housing while large numbers of dwellings stand empty or underused. In several metropolises, high-rise developments marketed to investors remain partially unlit; elsewhere, entire districts rise in anticipation of demand that never fully materialises. The paradox is stark: houses without people, and people without houses. In an age of industrialised real estate, financialised housing and ‘smart homes’ managed by algorithms, it may seem of little use to look instead toward the precarious self-built houses of the urban margins.

这种变化造成的后果清晰可见。在全世界，数以百万计的人艰难寻找稳定住所，与此同时，大量住宅却处于空置或利用不足的状态。在一些大都市里，面向投资者出售的高层住宅楼长期只有一部分窗户亮着灯；另一些地方，则有整片城区为了预计中的需求拔地而起，而这样的需求最终从未真正出现。这种悖论再鲜明不过：有房无人，有人无房。在房地产已经工业化、住房已经金融化、“智能住宅”又由算法进行管理的时代，把目光重新转向城市边缘那些脆弱不稳的自建房屋，似乎并没有多少用处。

Yet in self-building we encounter, perhaps more clearly than elsewhere, a form of practical wisdom – one that embodies and enacts the art of dwelling in the world as humans. This does not imply any idealisation of the poverty that often, though not invariably, accompanies processes of residential self-construction. Yet within conditions of housing precariousness there persists an everyday proximity to finitude – an awareness of fragility and contingency that stands as the counterpart to the human capacity to build a world: to carve out, patiently and imperfectly, a partially domesticated realm in which one may be at home.

然而，也许恰恰是在自建住房中，我们比其他任何地方都更清楚地遇见了一种实践智慧——一种体现并实际践行着“人如何栖居于世界”这门艺术的智慧。这并不意味着我们应当把自建住房过程中经常——但并非必然——相伴而生的贫困浪漫化。然而，在住房处境不稳定的条件下，人们的日常生活始终与有限性保持着一种贴近的关系：他们意识到生活的脆弱与偶然，而这种意识恰好对应着人类建造一个世界的能力——耐心地、不完美地，从世界中一点一点开辟出一个部分被驯化的领域，使人能够在其中安顿自身。

Self-construction serves as a reminder that any house, if it is truly to be lived as such, must in some sense be self-built. It must answer to a dwelling that precedes and exceeds it; it must sustain a life and secure, however provisionally, a place in the world. In *The Origin of the Work of Art* (1950), Heidegger asks what it means for something to be a ‘work’. He turns to the example of a Greek temple on a rocky promontory. The temple doesn’t merely occupy a site; it gathers a world. Birth and death, blessing and disaster, victory and defeat find their measure in its presence. To create a work, he suggests, is to set up a world – to bring earth into appearance and allow things to assume weight and distance within a shared horizon.

自建住房提醒我们：任何一栋房屋，如果真正要作为“房屋”而被人生活其中，那么从某种意义上说，它都必须是由居住者自己“建造”出来的。它必须回应一种先于它、同时又超越它的栖居；它必须承载一种生活，并且无论多么暂时，都要为人确保一个身处世界的位置。在 1950 年的《艺术作品的本源》中，海德格尔追问，一件东西成为“作品”究竟意味着什么。他举出岩石海岬上的一座希腊神庙作为例子。神庙并不只是占据了一个地点；它聚拢起一个世界。出生与死亡、祝福与灾难、胜利与失败，都在它的存在之中获得尺度。海德格尔提出，创造一件作品，就是建立一个世界——让大地显现出来，并使万物能够在一个共同的地平线之中获得自身的重量与距离。

The maternal house is one of the places from which a world first opens, what Gaston Bachelard once called the heart of the world for each person. It is there that we learn to distinguish near and far, intimate and foreign, belonging and estrangement. Walls retain memory; unfinished rooms carry expectation. In Heidegger’s evocation of the Black Forest hut, the house is not simply one place among others, but the place from which places are articulated – where earth and sky, mortals and divinities are gathered into a lived order.

母亲的家，是世界最初向一个人敞开的地方之一，也就是加斯东·巴什拉曾经称作对每个人而言的“世界之心”的地方。正是在那里，我们第一次学会区分远与近、亲密与陌生、归属与疏离。墙壁保存着记忆；尚未完成的房间承载着期待。在海德格尔对黑森林小屋的描绘中，房屋并不仅仅是众多地点中的一个，而是一个使其他地点得以彼此区分、获得意义的地方——在那里，大地与天空、凡人与神圣者被聚拢进一种实际生活着的秩序之中。

We spend our lives trying to make a home of the world. In this, we resemble Homer’s Odysseus: always on the way, exposed to contingency, yet oriented toward a place that might gather our wanderings into something like belonging. Ithaca is not only something left behind, a lost origin; it also lies ahead, as a horizon we must reclaim again and again. At the end of *The Odyssey*, Penelope, unable to recognise her husband in the aged stranger before her, proposes a test: have our marriage bed moved. It cannot be done, Odysseus replies, because he himself carved it from the trunk of a living olive tree rooted in the ground. The house was built around it. Penelope recognises him at once. What confirms his identity is not simply memory, but a shared act of making – earth worked into form, a dwelling raised around it, a life sedimented in wood and soil. The bed is rooted because it was made there; it binds land appropriated, craft undertaken and a bond forged over time.

我们一生都在尝试把这个世界变成一个家。在这一点上，我们很像荷马笔下的奥德修斯：永远身处旅途，永远暴露于偶然与变故之中，却始终朝向某个地方，希望那里能够将所有漂泊聚拢成某种近似于归属的东西。伊塔卡并不只是一个被抛在身后的地方、一个已经失去的起源；它也存在于前方，成为我们必须一次又一次重新抵达的地平线。在《奥德赛》的结尾，佩涅洛佩无法认出眼前这个衰老的陌生人就是自己的丈夫，于是提出一个考验：把我们的婚床搬走。奥德修斯回答说，那是不可能的，因为那张床是他亲手从一棵仍扎根于土地中的活橄榄树树干上雕刻出来的，整栋房屋都是围绕着它建起来的。佩涅洛佩立即认出了他。确认奥德修斯身份的并不只是记忆，而是一段共同的创造行为——土地被塑造成形，居所围绕它建立，一段人生沉积在木材与泥土之中。那张床之所以“扎根”，是因为它就在那里被制造出来；它把被取得的土地、付诸实践的手艺，以及随着时间逐渐锻造出来的人际纽带连接在一起。

This does not mean that only an immovable house can be a home. Many of us inhabit several dwellings over a lifetime; some are rented, others provisional, others left behind. Yet every house that becomes a home requires something akin to that rooted bed: an act of appropriation and recognition through which a portion of the world is made one’s own. Through such a gesture, even the most fragile dwelling can become the place where one learns to dwell.

这并不意味着，只有一栋固定不动的房屋才能成为家。我们许多人一生中都会居住在多个住所里；有些是租来的，有些只是临时住所，还有一些最终被我们留在身后。然而，每一栋真正成为“家”的房屋，都需要某种类似那张扎根婚床的东西：一种占有并辨认的行动，通过这种行动，世界的一部分被转化为属于自己的地方。正是通过这样的行动，即使最脆弱的住所，也能够成为一个人学习如何栖居的地方。
