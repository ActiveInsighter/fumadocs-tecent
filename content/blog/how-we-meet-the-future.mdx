*If we couldn’t make generalisations, we would be paralysed by the world’s complexity. Does that ever excuse stereotyping?*

*如果我们无法进行概括，就会被世界的复杂性弄得寸步难行。那么，这是否有时能够成为刻板印象的正当理由？*

Tom Ralston is the Jacobsen Postdoctoral Fellow at University College London and the Institute of Philosophy, University of London, where he researches the meaning and epistemology of generalisations.

Tom Ralston 是伦敦大学学院和伦敦大学哲学研究所的 Jacobsen 博士后研究员，他的研究聚焦于概括的意义及其认识论问题。

Overgeneralising has a bad rap. It seems an obvious, if understandable, mistake to generalise beyond what our evidence supports. A child playing in the park may assume, based on their experiences of their friendly pet golden retriever, that all dogs are friendly. But they would be wrong to approach an XL Bully, a breed of dog effectively outlawed in the UK recently following a string of attacks on children. When someone makes unwarranted generalisations about social groups, we condemn them for the sin of stereotyping. It is wrong to conclude after just one experience with a rude Brit that all Brits are rude.

过度概括的名声很不好。超出证据所支持的范围进行概括，看起来显然是一种错误，尽管这种错误可以理解。一个在公园里玩耍的孩子，也许会根据自己那只温顺友好的金毛寻回犬的经验，认为所有狗都很友善。但如果因此去接近一只 XL Bully 犬，那就错了——这种犬在英国近期发生一连串袭击儿童的事件后，实际上已遭到禁养。当一个人对社会群体作出毫无根据的概括时，我们会谴责其犯下了刻板印象的错误。仅仅因为遇到过一个粗鲁的英国人，就断定所有英国人都很粗鲁，显然是不对的。 

We hear less about the problems of *under*generalising. Generalising comes so naturally that few of us are at any risk of undergeneralising, so we give it little thought. The focus on overgeneralising is understandable given its potential costs, such as the child assuming that a dangerous dog is just like the friendly dogs they’ve encountered to date. Generalising itself, though, isn’t a bias that needs to be corrected. It is a cognitive shortcut and a fundamental part of our mental life. We’d be lost without it. It gives our experience structure and frees us up to focus on the new at the expense of the old. The child who approaches the XL Bully and the person who stereotypes Brits risk making a mistake, but the source of their error is not their desire to generalise.

相比之下，我们很少听人谈论“概括不足”的问题。概括对我们来说如此自然，以至于几乎没人会面临概括不足的风险，因此我们也很少考虑它。考虑到过度概括可能带来的代价，人们把注意力放在它身上是可以理解的——例如，那个孩子可能会认为一只危险的狗与自己此前遇到的友善狗没有区别。然而，概括本身并不是一种需要纠正的偏见。它是一种认知捷径，也是我们心理生活的基本组成部分。没有它，我们将无所适从。它为经验赋予结构，让我们能够减少对旧有信息的注意，把精力腾出来关注新事物。接近 XL Bully 犬的孩子，以及对英国人抱持刻板印象的人，都有犯错的风险，但他们错误的根源并不在于他们想要进行概括。 

What, then, is wrong with overgeneralising?

那么，过度概括究竟错在哪里？

Our story begins in Cambridge, 1930. Searching through the papers of his recently deceased friend, the philosopher Richard Braithwaite discovered a sheaf of handwritten notes on the nature of general beliefs, such as the belief that all humans are mortal. The friend was Frank Plumpton Ramsey, the remarkable philosopher and mathematician described as a ‘genius by all tests for genius’ by Paul Samuelson, the Nobel Laureate in economics. The notes were written in September 1929, just four months before Ramsey’s death from a mysterious illness that he may have contracted from swimming in the River Cam. Ramsey died aged just 26 but would go on to leave an oversized legacy in philosophy, mathematics, economics and logic.

我们的故事始于 1930 年的剑桥。哲学家 Richard Braithwaite 在整理一位刚刚去世的朋友留下的文件时，发现了一叠关于一般性信念本质的手写笔记，例如“所有人都会死亡”这样的信念。这位朋友就是 Frank Plumpton Ramsey——一位非凡的哲学家和数学家，诺贝尔经济学奖得主 Paul Samuelson 曾称他是“无论用什么天才标准衡量都称得上天才”的人物。这些笔记写于 1929 年 9 月，距离 Ramsey 因一种神秘疾病去世仅四个月；这种疾病可能是他在康河游泳时感染的。Ramsey 去世时年仅 26 岁，却在哲学、数学、经济学和逻辑学领域留下了远超其短暂生命长度的巨大遗产。

In those notes, later titled ‘General Propositions and Causality’ by Braithwaite, Ramsey advocated a view of generalisations that renounced both his own previous views and those of Ludwig Wittgenstein, on whom the young Ramsey, until only recently an undergraduate, exerted a significant influence during their time together in Cambridge in the late 1920s.

在这些后来被 Braithwaite 命名为《General Propositions and Causality》（《一般命题与因果性》）的笔记中，Ramsey 提出了一种新的概括理论。这种观点不仅抛弃了他自己先前的看法，也背离了 Ludwig Wittgenstein 的观点。20 世纪 20 年代末，两人在剑桥相处期间，刚刚本科毕业不久的年轻 Ramsey 曾对 Wittgenstein 产生相当大的影响。

Ramsey wanted to know what it means to say that a person believes that all humans are mortal. He concluded that, if you believe a generalisation, you have a habit or disposition. If you believe that all humans are mortal, you will take care not to run over your friends with your car and to make sure they have air to breathe, because you are disposed to treat any human you meet as mortal. Together, these habits and dispositions make up the system with which we ‘meet the future’. They are how we orient ourselves in the face of new observations and experiences.

Ramsey 想知道，当我们说某个人“相信所有人都会死亡”时，这究竟意味着什么。他得出的结论是：如果你相信某种概括，那么你就拥有一种相应的习惯或倾向。如果你相信所有人都会死亡，那么你会注意不要开车撞到朋友，也会确保他们有空气可以呼吸，因为你倾向于把遇见的任何一个人都当作会死亡的人来对待。所有这些习惯和倾向共同构成了一套让我们“迎接未来”的系统。面对新的观察和经验，我们正是依靠它们来确定自己的行动方向。

> Two people can disagree in their beliefs about generalisations, even when they agree on all the facts about particulars

> 即便两个人对所有具体事实都意见一致，他们对于概括性信念仍然可能存在分歧。

What is the alternative? Wittgenstein, with whose writing Ramsey was very familiar, having worked on the translation of the fiendishly cryptic *Tractatus Logico-Philosophicus* (1921) from German into English while still an undergraduate, had argued that to believe a generalisation is to believe an infinite conjunction of facts about particulars: Alan is mortal and Bella is mortal and Carl is mortal and so on for every human being you could possibly mention. Ramsey’s new view saw beliefs in generalisations as involving a disposition to treat any human you encounter as mortal. That’s a bit different. After all, the disposition is a good one to the extent that it gets things right when tested. The disposition to treat any human I meet as mortal has not led me astray so far, but the disposition to treat any human as having blonde hair would falter quickly. That is why I don’t believe that all humans have blonde hair.

另一种观点是什么呢？Ramsey 对 Wittgenstein 的著作非常熟悉——本科期间，他就参与了那部晦涩得令人头疼的《逻辑哲学论》（*Tractatus Logico-Philosophicus*, 1921）从德语到英语的翻译工作。Wittgenstein 曾主张，相信一个概括，就是相信有关具体个体的无限多个事实的合取：Alan 会死，Bella 会死，Carl 会死……一直延伸到你可能说出的每一个人。Ramsey 的新观点则认为，相信一种概括意味着你具有某种倾向，例如把你遇到的任何一个人都当作会死亡的人来对待。两者并不完全一样。毕竟，一种倾向究竟是不是好的，要看它在接受检验时能多大程度上得出正确结果。迄今为止，把我遇到的任何人都视为会死亡的人，并没有让我误入歧途；但如果我倾向于认为任何人都是金发，这种倾向很快就会碰壁。因此，我并不相信所有人都有金发。

Why did Ramsey reject his earlier view? Ramsey saw that two people can disagree in their beliefs about generalisations, even when they agree on all the facts about particulars. Imagine that I am debating a techno-utopian who believes that mortality is only a contingent feature of humanity’s present existence and that, in principle, humans could live forever. I can agree with them that every human up to now has been mortal, but at the same time disagree over the question of whether all humans are mortal. The techno-utopian would disagree with me that all humans are mortal, even though no human has yet achieved immortality. If believing the generalisation involved believing only that each previous human had been mortal, we could not disagree without disagreeing about a particular human’s mortality. But we agree about all past humans having been mortal. That’s not contested. We disagree only about possible future humans, without disagreeing about any particular human. My disposition to treat humans as mortal would come unstuck in a possible future in which some tech wizard unlocks the secret of immortality. Our disagreement does not concern the facts about the mortality of any actual human, but rather the possible future applications of the disposition.

Ramsey 为什么会否定自己早先的观点？因为他意识到，即使两个人对所有具体个体的事实都没有分歧，他们仍然可以在概括性信念上意见不同。设想我正在和一个技术乌托邦主义者争论。对方认为，死亡只是人类当前生存状态中的一种偶然特征，从原则上说，人完全有可能实现永生。我完全可以同意他所说的“迄今为止每一个人都会死亡”，却同时不同意他关于“是否所有人都必然会死亡”的观点。尽管目前还没有任何人获得永生，这位技术乌托邦主义者仍可以否认“所有人都会死亡”。如果相信这一概括仅仅意味着相信过去每一个具体的人都已经或终将死亡，那么只要我们对任何具体个人的死亡事实没有分歧，就不可能在这个概括上产生分歧。但事实上，我们都同意过去所有人都是会死亡的。这一点并无争议。我们的分歧仅仅涉及未来可能出现的人，而不涉及任何一个现实中的具体个人。如果未来某位科技奇才能够破解永生的秘密，那么我“把人类都视为会死亡”的倾向就会失效。因此，我们争论的并不是任何现实人物是否会死亡的事实，而是这种倾向在未来可能如何适用。

This new perspective helps to explain why Ramsey revised his earlier view of generalisations. Rather than asking *What does the world need to be like for a belief in a generalisation to be true?*, Ramsey asked *What is a belief in a generalisation for?* His answer, as I’ve already suggested, is: *to meet the future*. Why? Human experience is far too complex for us to attend to every detail. We must be selective in the things we pay attention to. But how do we decide what to focus on and what to ignore? Beliefs in generalisations help to solve this dilemma. When I see a double-decker red vehicle in London, I can use my general knowledge about buses to predict that it is going to pull in at the next bus stop to pick up the person signalling with their arm. I don’t have to wait to see what the vehicle does, which frees me up to focus my attention elsewhere. These predictions don’t need to be infallible to be useful. When a bus is full or off duty, it won’t stop for passengers. Even if my beliefs sometimes lead me to make a mistake, I use them in generalisations to make predictions about what the future will be like, and I use these predictions to decide what to ignore.

这一新视角可以帮助我们理解，Ramsey 为什么修改了自己先前关于概括的理论。他不再追问：“世界必须是什么样子，我们对某种概括的信念才算是真的？”而是问：“我们相信一种概括，究竟是为了什么？”正如前面所说，他的回答是：**为了迎接未来。**为什么？因为人类经验实在太复杂，我们根本无法关注其中的每一个细节。我们不得不有所选择。但我们怎样判断应该关注什么、忽略什么？概括性信念恰恰可以帮助解决这个难题。当我在伦敦看见一辆红色双层车辆时，可以凭借自己关于公交车的一般知识，预测它会驶入下一个公交站，去接那个正在挥手示意的人。我不需要等到这辆车真正采取行动，因而可以把注意力转移到其他地方。这样的预测并不需要百发百中才具有价值。一辆公交车如果已经满员或者停止运营，就不会停车载客。即使我的信念偶尔会让我判断错误，我仍然利用这些概括来预测未来会是什么样子，并根据这些预测决定哪些事情可以忽略。

Not all our generalisations are put to the same use. For Ramsey, whether we should believe a generalisation depends on the virtues of the habit that constitutes the belief. Universal generalisations admit no exceptions: upon meeting a single hairless Sphynx cat, I should no longer believe that all cats are furry. This feature of universal generalisations means that, if they are to be useful, they must be formulated with a high degree of precision. My belief that all cats are furry is too coarse-grained. I should perhaps have believed that all cats except Sphynx cats are furry instead. But even that won’t be quite right, once I learn about Ukrainian Levkoys. And, as they become more precise, these beliefs become less useful, because they become more complex, with too many ad hoc clauses. We go from the simple ‘All cats are furry’ to ‘All cats except Sphynx cats and Ukrainian Levkoys and this one and that one… are furry.’

并不是所有概括都有相同的用途。对 Ramsey 来说，我们是否应该相信某个概括，要看构成这种信念的习惯本身是否良好。**全称概括**不允许任何例外：只要遇见一只没有毛的斯芬克斯猫，我就不应该再相信“所有猫都有毛”。全称概括的这个特点意味着，要想让它们真正有用，就必须把它们表述得极其精确。“所有猫都有毛”这个信念实在过于粗糙。或许我应该改为相信“除了斯芬克斯猫以外，所有猫都有毛”。但当我进一步了解到乌克兰列夫科伊猫之后，这句话也不再完全正确。随着信念变得越来越精确，它们反而会越来越没用，因为内容也越来越复杂，需要增加大量临时性的附加条件。于是，我们从简单的“所有猫都有毛”，逐渐变成“除了斯芬克斯猫、乌克兰列夫科伊猫，还有这一只、那一只……之外，所有猫都有毛”。

*Sphinx cat. Courtesy Wikimedia.*

*斯芬克斯无毛猫。图片来源：Wikimedia。*

Ramsey, a mathematically inclined philosopher, was interested in precisely formulated universal generalisations. But many of the generalisations that we believe don’t demand this kind of precision. I believe that cats are furry. How many hairless cats do I have to meet before I should abandon this belief? Generalisations like ‘Cats are furry’, without a quantifying word such as ‘all’ or ‘most’, are now known to philosophers and linguists as generic generalisations. They are about what is generally true of a category (cats), but do not say what proportion of the category has the property in question (being furry). I can answer the question *How many cats are furry?* by saying *Most cats are furry*, but not just *Cats are furry*. These are more common in everyday life than the universal generalisations that philosophers and logicians like to deal with.

Ramsey 是一位数学气质很强的哲学家，因此他感兴趣的是表述精确的全称概括。但我们实际相信的许多概括，并不需要这种精确程度。我相信“猫是有毛的”。那么，我究竟需要遇见多少只无毛猫，才应该放弃这个信念？像“猫是有毛的”这样没有“所有”或“大多数”等量词的概括，如今被哲学家和语言学家称作**泛称概括（generic generalisations）**。它们描述的是某个类别——例如猫——通常具有怎样的性质，却不说明这个类别中究竟有多大比例的成员具有这种性质——例如有毛。如果有人问“有多少猫是有毛的？”，我可以回答“大多数猫都有毛”，却不能只回答“猫有毛”。这种泛称概括在我们的日常生活中，比哲学家和逻辑学家偏爱处理的全称概括普遍得多。

> My disposition to treat any cat as furry can lead me to error, but it gets things right enough of the time

> 我把任何猫都当作有毛动物的倾向，的确可能使我犯错，但它在足够多的时候都能让我判断正确。

As with the universal generalisations Ramsey discussed, to believe a generic generalisation is to be disposed to treat members of a category as having some property. Where they differ is that generic beliefs need not be revised in the face of one or even many counterexamples. I can hold on to my belief that cats are furry in the knowledge that many cats have no fur at all. I don’t need to revise my belief that buses pull in to pick up passengers just because they don’t when they are full. Things can get even more extreme: as the philosopher and cognitive scientist Sarah-Jane Leslie has argued, people will accept that mosquitoes carry the West Nile virus even when they are aware that only 1 per cent of mosquitoes are carriers. Sometimes, an incredibly strong correlation is not enough to warrant a belief in a generic generalisation: I don’t believe that humans are right-handed, even though around 90 per cent of us are.

与 Ramsey 所讨论的全称概括一样，相信一个泛称概括，也意味着我们倾向于把某个类别的成员当作具有某种性质的事物来对待。两者的区别在于：即使面对一个甚至许多个反例，泛称信念也不一定需要修改。我完全可以在知道很多猫根本没有毛的情况下，继续相信“猫是有毛的”。我也不需要仅仅因为公交车满员时不会停车载客，就修改“公交车会进站接乘客”的信念。情况甚至可以更加极端：正如哲学家兼认知科学家 Sarah-Jane Leslie 所指出的，即便人们知道只有 1% 的蚊子携带西尼罗河病毒，他们依然会接受“蚊子携带西尼罗河病毒”这一说法。有时候，即使存在极强的统计相关性，也不足以让我们接受一个泛称概括：例如，大约 90% 的人都是右利手，但我并不会因此相信“人类是右利手的”。 

All of this makes it extremely difficult to answer the question: what does the world need to be like for a generic generalisation to be true? Taking inspiration from Ramsey, we can ask instead: what are generic generalisations for? They are generalisations about a category, but they don’t apply univocally to every member of the category. Because they are tolerant of exceptions, they demand less of us than universal generalisations do. They don’t require us to revise our beliefs in the face of counterexamples. This surprising and rarely mentioned feature is what makes them so useful: I can form many generic beliefs about categories without worrying about getting things precisely right in every case. My disposition to treat any cat I meet as furry will sometimes lead me to error, but it is still worth holding on to that disposition because it gets things right enough of the time. And when I get things wrong, it is unlikely that there will be much of a cost. Before encountering it, I might mistakenly assume that my friend’s new cat will be furry and then discover that it is a Sphynx cat, but errors of this kind are not going to keep me up at night.

所有这些都让一个问题变得极其难以回答：世界究竟必须是什么样子，一个泛称概括才能算是真的？受 Ramsey 启发，我们不妨换一个问题：泛称概括究竟有什么用？它们是关于某一类别的概括，却不会以完全相同的方式适用于这个类别中的每一个成员。正因为它们允许例外，所以对我们的要求低于全称概括。即使出现反例，我们也不必立刻修改信念。这个令人意外、却很少有人提到的特点，恰恰是泛称概括如此有用的原因：我们可以对各种类别形成大量泛称信念，而不必担心自己是否在每一种情况下都精确无误。我倾向于把遇见的任何一只猫都看作有毛，这偶尔会让我判断错误，但这种倾向仍值得保留，因为它在足够多的时候都能得到正确结果。而且，即使判断错了，代价通常也不会太高。我可能在见到朋友的新猫之前错误地以为它会有毛，结果见面后发现那是一只斯芬克斯无毛猫，但这种错误还不至于让我夜不能寐。

So far, so good for generic generalisations. They simplify the world by abstracting away from individual differences. They are insensitive to proportions, so we don’t need precise statistical evidence to support them. They admit exceptions so we don’t continually need to revise them. But the very features that make generic generalisations so useful are also hallmarks of stereotypes. Universal generalisations don’t make for good stereotypes – they are too easy to falsify by finding a single counterexample. Generic generalisations are better suited to this task, since we can hold on to them even when we know of a large number of counterexamples.

到目前为止，泛称概括看起来都很好。它们通过抽掉个体差异来简化世界；它们对比例并不敏感，因此我们不需要精确的统计证据来支持它们；它们允许存在例外，所以我们也不必不断修改自己的信念。然而，让泛称概括如此有用的那些特征，同时也是**刻板印象**的典型特征。全称概括并不适合构成刻板印象，因为只要找到一个反例，就很容易把它推翻。泛称概括则更适合承担这一角色，因为即使我们知道存在大量反例，仍然可以继续保留它。

To stereotype a category is to associate certain properties with the category and its members. Like other beliefs in generalisations, stereotypes are part of the system with which we meet the future. They impose structure on our experiences. We cannot attend to every individual difference but instead use these beliefs to simplify our experience and make predictions about the future. And we do not revise our beliefs in these generalisations every time we meet an exception, for fear of abandoning the structure they provide.

对一个类别形成刻板印象，就是把某些性质与这个类别及其成员联系起来。和其他概括性信念一样，刻板印象也是我们用来“迎接未来”的系统的一部分。它们为我们的经验赋予结构。我们无法关注每一个个体之间的全部差异，因此会利用这些信念简化经验，并对未来作出预测。而每当遇见一个例外时，我们也不会立刻修改这些概括性信念，因为那样做可能会让我们失去它们所提供的结构。

Stereotyping social groups, including those defined by nationality, gender or skin colour, is typically thought morally wrong, because it undermines people’s autonomy by focusing on their membership of a group, rather than their qualities as individuals. It is common to hear that stereotyping is not only a moral failing, but an *epistemic* one, a failure of rationality or to fulfil our duties as knowers. We are sometimes even told that a tendency to stereotype is a consequence of low cognitive ability.

对于由国籍、性别或肤色等因素界定的社会群体形成刻板印象，通常被认为在道德上是错误的，因为这种做法把注意力集中在一个人的群体身份，而不是他作为独立个体所具有的品质，从而损害了人的自主性。我们经常听到一种说法：刻板印象不仅是一种道德失败，也是一种**认识论上的失败**——它意味着理性上的失败，或者意味着我们没有履行自己作为认知主体的责任。有时甚至有人声称，容易形成刻板印象是认知能力较低造成的。

There are many ways that stereotyping might contribute to an epistemic failure. It often leads us to error, in part because the evidence from which we generalise is bad. Stereotypical portrayals of members of historically oppressed ethnic groups in film and television can form the basis for stereotypical generalisations about those groups, which in turn perpetuate the stereotype in future portrayals. Stereotyping might cause us to systematically misinterpret ambiguous evidence in a way that leads us to develop pernicious false beliefs. Black people in the UK are significantly more likely to be stopped and searched by police officers than white people. Holding a racial stereotype about that group might lead a person to conclude incorrectly that its members have a higher innate propensity to commit crimes.

刻板印象可以通过许多方式造成认识论上的失败。它往往会把我们引向错误，其中一个原因在于，我们进行概括时所依据的证据本身就很糟糕。影视作品如果以刻板方式描绘历史上遭受压迫的族群成员，就可能成为人们对这些群体形成刻板概括的依据；而这些概括又会反过来影响未来的影视描绘，从而不断延续这种刻板印象。刻板印象还可能让我们系统性地错误解释含糊的证据，最终形成有害的错误信念。在英国，黑人被警察拦下搜查的概率明显高于白人。一个人如果对这个群体抱有种族刻板印象，就可能错误地推断说，该群体成员天生就更倾向于犯罪。

> Generalising means meeting the future with assumptions that make the world around us intelligible

> 进行概括，意味着带着一些能够让周围世界变得可以理解的预设去迎接未来。

Important though these troubling aspects of stereotyping are, I am more interested in the question of whether there is something inherently irrational in the epistemic attitude that leads to stereotyping.

尽管刻板印象的这些问题十分重要，但我更感兴趣的是另一个问题：导致我们形成刻板印象的那种认识态度，本身是否就具有某种内在的非理性？

To answer that question, we must consider why we stereotype at all. Stereotyping is a form of generalising. People stereotype because they need to simplify their experience. And because we all need to simplify our experience, we should not rule out stereotyping as inherently irrational. It may often lead us to error, but our mistake is not our desire to generalise.

要回答这个问题，我们必须先考虑：我们究竟为什么会形成刻板印象？刻板印象本身就是概括的一种形式。人之所以会形成刻板印象，是因为我们需要简化自己的经验。而既然我们所有人都必须简化经验，就不能直接把刻板印象判定为一种本质上非理性的行为。它确实常常会使我们犯错，但真正的错误并不在于我们想进行概括这件事本身。

Human cognition is limited. That is why mental shortcuts like generalising are an essential part of our mental lives. In Daniel Kahneman’s 2002 Nobel Prize-winning work on bounded rationality, summarised in his *Thinking, Fast and Slow* (2011) and originally developed with Amos Tversky, he found that people employ a range of cognitive heuristics when asked to make complex judgments. Heuristics are ways of making decisions under conditions of uncertainty or limited resources. Because they allow us to ignore a lot of information, heuristics are not always accurate. In a 1983 study by Kahneman and Tversky illustrating the ‘conjunction fallacy’, people were presented with the following vignette:

人类的认知能力是有限的。因此，像概括这样的心理捷径，是我们心理生活中不可或缺的一部分。Daniel Kahneman 因有限理性研究获得了 2002 年诺贝尔经济学奖；这些研究后来被总结在他的《思考，快与慢》（*Thinking, Fast and Slow*, 2011）一书中，而最初则是他与 Amos Tversky 共同发展起来的。Kahneman 发现，当人们被要求作出复杂判断时，会使用各种各样的认知启发式方法。所谓启发式，就是人在不确定或资源有限的条件下作出决策的方法。由于启发式允许我们忽略大量信息，它们并不总是准确。Kahneman 和 Tversky 在 1983 年一项用于展示“合取谬误”的研究中，向参与者提供了下面这段描述：

> Linda is 31 years old, single, outspoken and very bright. She majored in philosophy. As a student, she was deeply concerned with issues of discrimination and social justice, and also participated in anti-nuclear demonstrations.

> Linda 31 岁，单身，直率，而且非常聪明。她大学主修哲学。学生时代，她非常关心歧视和社会正义问题，同时还参加过反核示威活动。

Their participants, including those with sophisticated knowledge of statistics, judged ‘Linda is a bank teller and is active in the feminist movement’ to be more probable than ‘Linda is a bank teller’, given the information in the vignette. But the former entails the latter and so cannot be more probable. Instead of applying precise statistical reasoning, people are employing a ‘representativeness’ heuristic, where the first option is judged more probable than the second because the description of Linda is more representative of their idea of a feminist bank teller than of a bank teller. It is tempting to interpret this as evidence of widespread irrationality, but to dismiss these heuristics out of hand is to ignore the value of such cognitive shortcuts. We often need to reason quickly or with only partial evidence, and heuristics are invaluable in such circumstances. A person who never employed heuristic thinking would be overwhelmed by competing cognitive demands. Heuristics, though fallible in systematic ways, are essential tools for navigating the complexity of experience.

根据这段描述，实验参与者——其中甚至包括具有相当成熟统计学知识的人——认为“Linda 是一名银行出纳员，同时积极参与女权运动”比“Linda 是一名银行出纳员”更有可能。但前一个命题蕴含后一个命题，因此前者在概率上不可能高于后者。人们并没有进行精确的统计推理，而是采用了一种“代表性启发法”：第一个选项之所以被认为更可能，是因为 Linda 的描述更符合人们心目中“参与女权运动的银行出纳员”的典型形象，而不像一般的“银行出纳员”。我们很容易把这种现象解释为人类普遍非理性的证据，但如果就此彻底否定启发式，就忽略了这些认知捷径的价值。我们经常需要快速推理，或者只能依据不完整的证据进行判断，在这种情况下启发式方法极其宝贵。一个从不进行启发式思考的人，会被各种相互竞争的认知任务彻底淹没。尽管启发式会以某些系统性的方式出错，它们仍然是我们应对复杂经验不可缺少的工具。

Generalising is another kind of cognitive shortcut. Like Tversky and Kahneman’s heuristics, it may lead us to errors. This is because generalising inherently goes beyond our evidence. My dispute with the techno-utopian over the mortality of humans concerns what is possible for humans; we don’t disagree about the evidence that is available.

概括是另一种认知捷径。与 Tversky 和 Kahneman 所研究的启发式一样，它同样可能让我们犯错。这是因为概括在本质上就会超出我们已有的证据。我与那个技术乌托邦主义者关于人类是否必然死亡的争论，涉及的是“人类可能成为什么样”，而我们对于现有证据本身其实并没有分歧。

Generalising means meeting the future with assumptions that make the world around us intelligible, but at the expense of paying less attention to individual differences. Stereotyping is a way of making the social world intelligible. That does not make stereotyping right. It can be accurate or wildly inaccurate, and it lends itself to abuse. It can harm members of stereotyped groups in many ways, in part because it is a failure to see people as individuals, rather than simply as members of a category. The cognitive utility of stereotyping does not make it rational: holding on to a stereotype in the face of overwhelming evidence against it is surely irrational. But it does make stereotyping understandable and perhaps even morally excusable, since it reflects our need to make predictions about the future, based on the categories that things, including people, fall into.

进行概括，就是带着一组能够使周围世界变得可理解的预设去迎接未来，但代价是我们会减少对个体差异的关注。刻板印象正是一种使社会世界变得可理解的方法。这当然不意味着刻板印象就是正确的。它可能相当准确，也可能错得离谱，而且极易被滥用。它能够以许多方式伤害被刻板化群体的成员，其中部分原因就在于，它没有把人看作独立个体，而只是把他们看作某个类别的成员。刻板印象具有认知上的用途，并不意味着它就是理性的：面对压倒性的反面证据仍然坚持某种刻板印象，显然是不理性的。但这种认知用途确实让刻板印象变得可以理解，甚至在某些情况下可能在道德上得到一定程度的宽宥，因为它反映了我们根据事物——包括人——所属的类别来预测未来的基本需要。

How do we find a balance between undergeneralising and overgeneralising? One place to start is to seek out counterexamples to our generalisations. We can then rule out at least some of our general beliefs as false. This response to the problem of generalisations was proposed by the philosopher of science Karl Popper. He famously argued that, strictly speaking, we are never justified in believing that universal generalisations are true on the basis of experience, since there is always another potential counterexample lurking beyond our observation. We are, however, justified in believing that a universal generalisation is false when we know of a single counterexample. A single immortal human would falsify the generalisation that all humans are mortal.

那么，我们怎样才能在概括不足与过度概括之间找到平衡？一个起点，就是主动寻找我们的概括所面临的反例。这样一来，我们至少可以排除一部分错误的一般性信念。科学哲学家 Karl Popper 曾提出过这种应对概括问题的方法。他有一个著名主张：严格来说，我们永远无法仅仅根据经验，就获得相信一个全称概括为真的充分理由，因为在我们的观察范围之外，总有可能潜伏着另一个反例。但是，只要我们知道一个反例，就有充分理由相信某个全称概括是假的。只需要出现一个永生的人，就足以证伪“所有人都会死亡”这一概括。

This proposal works well enough for universal generalisations, since we can easily state the conditions in which they are false. Popper’s concern was the practice of science, where the precision of universal generalisations is welcome. But many of our everyday general beliefs are not beliefs in universal generalisations. They are, instead, generic beliefs. The problem with these beliefs is that neither ordinary speakers nor trained linguists have a clear idea of the conditions under which generic generalisations are true or false. They are tolerant of exceptions, so the mere fact that there are some hairless cats does not mean my belief that cats are furry is false. And if we can’t say when they are false, then Popper’s suggested search for counterevidence will not tell us what (not) to believe.

这种方案用来处理全称概括时效果相当不错，因为我们很容易明确说出它们在什么条件下是假的。Popper 主要关心的是科学实践，而在科学中，全称概括所要求的精确性恰恰非常受欢迎。但是，我们日常生活中的许多一般性信念并不是全称概括，而是泛称信念。这类信念的问题在于：无论普通说话者还是受过专业训练的语言学家，都无法清楚说明泛称概括究竟在什么条件下为真或为假。它们允许例外，所以仅仅存在一些无毛猫，并不能证明“猫有毛”这一信念是假的。如果我们连它们什么时候算假都无法确定，那么 Popper 所建议的寻找反证的方法，就无法告诉我们究竟应该相信什么、又不应该相信什么。 

Without clear criteria for truth and falsity, how can we say what evidence should lead us to revise our generic beliefs? Here’s my answer. We should, like Ramsey, approach this question pragmatically. There are two factors that we should take into account when evaluating our generic beliefs. Both rest on Ramsey’s idea that beliefs in generalisations are dispositions to be judged by their consequences. For any particular generic generalisation we should ask two questions. First, how often does it lead us into error? Second, how costly are the consequences of these errors? Our replies will not give us a definitive answer, as Popper hoped, to the question of whether we should revise our beliefs in generalisations. But, if we follow Popper’s advice and actively seek out counterexamples, and try to falsify them to test their strength, then they can act as a guide, helping us to weigh up the importance of getting things right and the consequences of getting things wrong.

既然没有明确的真假标准，我们又该如何判断，什么样的证据应该促使我们修改自己的泛称信念？我的回答是：我们应该像 Ramsey 那样，以一种实用主义方式处理这个问题。在评价一个泛称信念时，需要考虑两个因素。这两个因素都建立在 Ramsey 的观点之上：概括性信念是一种倾向，而评价这种倾向应该看它造成的后果。对于任何一个具体的泛称概括，我们都应该问两个问题。第一，它多么频繁地让我们犯错？第二，这些错误造成的后果代价有多大？这些问题的答案，并不能像 Popper 所希望的那样，明确告诉我们究竟是否应该修改某个概括性信念。但如果我们遵循 Popper 的建议，主动寻找反例，并尝试证伪自己的信念，以检验它们究竟有多坚固，那么这两个问题就能够发挥指导作用，帮助我们权衡：判断正确有多重要，以及判断错误又会造成什么后果。

> My generic beliefs about social categories need to meet a higher standard than those about mosquitoes

> 与关于蚊子的泛称信念相比，我对社会类别所抱持的泛称信念需要达到更高的标准。

If I believe that mosquitoes carry the West Nile virus, I am likely to be wrong most of the time when I judge individual mosquitoes to be carriers of the disease. But if I do get bitten by a mosquito carrying the virus, I may develop a serious illness. Although I am likely to be wrong a lot of the time, my error is worth it because I will take precautions against all mosquitoes, infected or not. This reduces my likelihood of contracting the disease.

如果我相信“蚊子携带西尼罗河病毒”，那么当我把一只具体的蚊子判断为病毒携带者时，大多数情况下很可能都是错的。但如果真的被一只携带病毒的蚊子叮咬，我却可能患上严重疾病。因此，即使这种判断常常是错误的，它依然值得保留，因为它会促使我对所有蚊子——无论是否感染病毒——都采取防范措施，从而降低自己感染疾病的概率。

Next, consider a stereotype about a social group: Scottish people drink too much. According to a press release summarising a 2025 report from Public Health Scotland, ‘people in Scotland are drinking 50 per cent above safe limits’. The stereotype may lead me to make accurate judgments in many cases but, when we consider the costs of the errors that I do make, my failure to treat those Scots as individuals and focus on them and their personal circumstances harms them. Indeed, this failure is sometimes claimed to constitute the intrinsic wrong of stereotyping. The costs of my errors need not be borne by me for me to take them into account. My generic beliefs about social categories may, therefore, need to meet a higher standard than those about, say, mosquitoes, since there is more at stake, morally speaking, when I make errors about other people.

接下来考虑一个针对社会群体的刻板印象：“苏格兰人喝酒过量。”根据一份概述苏格兰公共卫生部门 2025 年报告的新闻稿，“苏格兰人的饮酒量比安全限值高出 50%”。这种刻板印象在许多情况下也许确实能让我作出正确判断，但当我们考虑那些判断错误所造成的代价时，情况就不同了：如果我没有把那些苏格兰人当作独立个体来看待，没有关注他们本人以及他们各自的具体处境，我就可能伤害他们。事实上，有人认为，这种失败正是刻板印象本质上的道德错误所在。即使错误的代价并不是由我本人承担，我仍然应该把这些代价纳入考虑。因此，我对于社会类别的泛称信念，也许应该达到比“蚊子”之类对象更高的标准，因为当我对其他人作出错误判断时，从道德角度来看，所牵涉的利害关系要大得多。

The child who approaches the XL Bully expecting it to behave like their friendly golden retriever is making a dangerous mistake because they have overgeneralised from their experiences with their tame pet. The costs of that mistake might be very high. But what this example obscures is that many of the generalisations that the child makes on the basis of their experiences with their pet are true: dogs bark, dogs have four legs, dogs eat meat, dogs are furry. If the child failed to draw those conclusions from their admittedly meagre evidence, their knowledge of the world would be severely impoverished. What matters is that the child tests these generalisations against new evidence. And while it might be reckless, it is by approaching the XL Bully that they learn of their mistake. Popper would have approved.

那个接近 XL Bully 犬、并期待它像自己温顺友好的金毛犬一样行动的孩子，犯了一个危险的错误，因为他把自己与家养宠物相处的经验过度概括到了其他狗身上。这种错误的代价可能非常高。但这个例子掩盖了一个事实：孩子根据自己养狗经验作出的很多概括其实都是正确的——狗会叫，狗有四条腿，狗吃肉，狗身上有毛。如果孩子因为自己的证据确实十分有限，就拒绝得出这些结论，那么他对世界的认识反而会变得极其贫乏。真正重要的是，孩子应该用新的证据不断检验这些概括。虽然接近那只 XL Bully 犬的做法也许相当鲁莽，但恰恰是在接近它之后，孩子才发现自己的概括出了问题。Popper 大概会对此表示赞许。

Our need to generalise is in tension with the requirement that we respect each other’s autonomy. This is a tension between a cognitive obligation – to simplify experience and so make it comprehensible – and a moral one – to treat one another as individuals in all their rich specificity. It is easy to confuse a moral failing for a cognitive one but, when we criticise someone for overgeneralising or even stereotyping, we should remember how essential that cognitive shortcut is. Even stereotypes are part of the system with which we meet the future. To abandon that system wholesale would be to lose the in-built tools that enable us to focus on what is truly new.

我们进行概括的需要，与尊重彼此自主性的要求之间存在张力。这是一种**认知义务**与**道德义务**之间的冲突：前者要求我们简化经验，从而使经验变得可以理解；后者则要求我们把彼此当作具有丰富而独特具体性的独立个体来对待。我们很容易把一种道德失败误认为认知失败。但是，当我们批评一个人过度概括，甚至形成刻板印象时，也应该记住，这种认知捷径对于人类来说究竟有多么重要。即便是刻板印象，也是我们用来迎接未来的那套系统的一部分。如果把整个系统彻底抛弃，我们也就失去了那些内置于认知之中、使我们能够把注意力集中到真正新事物上的工具。
